# [Modulation Identification] Automated Modulation Identification of Radio Frequency Proximity Sensor Signals Based on an Adaptive Correlational Graph Attention Network with Python Code Included

Introduction  
The Principles of Operation for Radio Frequency Proximity Sensors (RFPS) are similar to radar, primarily used for target detection and providing control information [1]. Due to their efficiency and low cost, RFPS are widely used in commercial and military applications, playing a crucial role in short-range target detection tasks. In these scenarios, the accurate and precise identification of RFPS signals is essential, as it directly affects the accuracy of target detection and the overall stability and reliability of the system.  
To achieve accurate identification of RFPS signals, Automated Modulation Recognition (AMR) technology has become a key solution. AMR is a technique that automatically recognizes the modulation type of received signals without prior information [4]. It is widely used in modern electronic warfare and cognitive radio systems for signal reconnaissance and sensing in cooperative and non-cooperative environments, playing a critical role in threat perception. However, RFPS signals vary greatly, and due to their short-range working characteristics, the transmitted signals typically have low power, operating at K-band or higher millimeter wave bands. High-frequency signals undergo significant attenuation during transmission, leading to lower Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) in the antenna, especially in harsh remote reconnaissance environments where SNR further deteriorates [9]. This poses a significant challenge for AMR of RFPS signals in such conditions. Therefore, achieving AMR in complex environments under low SNR conditions is crucial for remote reconnaissance and signal recognition.
Traditional AMR methods typically fall into two main categories: maximum likelihood ratio methods based on hypothesis testing and feature extraction methods based on expertise [10]. The maximum likelihood ratio method employs Bayesian theory to identify signals by calculating the likelihood ratio of the received signal through computation. Although this method is theoretically optimal, its severe reliance on prior information and high computational complexity limit its practical applicability [12]. Conversely, feature extraction methods rely on manually designed features and classifiers constructed using traditional machine learning algorithms [13]. These features include instantaneous amplitude, frequency, phase, constellation diagram features, higher-order statistical features, and cyclic stationary features. However, these methods largely depend on human expertise and involve complex feature extraction processes, presenting difficulties in determining empirical thresholds in dynamic environments. Common classifiers include support vector machines (SVM), K-means, decision trees, and artificial neural networks (ANN). Classification performance significantly deteriorates when the signal-to-noise ratio is low, nonlinear interference exists, parameters are highly intertwined, or high-dimensional features are required.
Compared with traditional methods, deep learning-based AMR methods exhibit higher efficiency in complex open environments [22]. By leveraging deep neural networks, these methods can automatically extract signal features, significantly reducing the reliance on manually designed features and demonstrating outstanding performance under low SNR conditions [23].
The application of deep learning to AMR tasks under low SNR conditions has emerged as a prominent research direction. For instance, in [23], continuous wavelet transform (CWT) was applied to the received signal to generate 2-D time-frequency images, which were then classified using convolutional neural networks (CNNs), enabling effective identification of 12 modulation types across different SNR levels. The experimental results showed that even at an SNR as low as −11 dB, this method achieved a classification accuracy of 90%. In [24], an efficient AMR approach was proposed, integrating short-time Fourier transform (STFT) and deep learning techniques with multiscale expansion pyramid module (MDPM). This module preserved feature resolution while capturing multiscale information, significantly enhancing robustness under low SNR conditions while ensuring low computational costs for real-time applications on resource-constrained devices. In [25], a data reordering and two-dimensional fast Fourier transform (DR2D) preprocessing method for modulation feature extraction was introduced, and a DenseNet feature extraction network with early fusion was used to identify these features, achieving high recognition accuracy in low SNR environments.
Despite the use of deep learning networks as classifiers, data preprocessing still relies on manually designed features. To further reduce reliance on prior knowledge, some researchers propose directly inputting time-series data to simplify signal preprocessing processes. In [26], IQ time-domain signals from multiple radiation sources were fed into a CNN model for modulation recognition, with results showing that the recognition accuracy exceeded 65% when SNR was above −5 dB. In [27], an approach based on a 2-D data matrix and an improved residual network for modulation recognition was proposed, significantly improving recognition accuracy under low SNR conditions. Similarly, in [28], an AMR method using ResNeXt-GRU networks was introduced, which improved classification accuracy in low SNR environments through deep feature fusion.
Compared to CNN, graph neural networks (GNN) exhibit outstanding capabilities in capturing local features and global relationships. In recent years, GNNs have made significant progress in processing and analyzing non-stationary signals. Due to the rich graph structure data and mature end-to-end learning strategies of GNNs, applying them to AMR has become an important research focus. One method is to convert manually designed signal features into graph structure data and then process it using GNN for recognition. In [31], time-series signals were converted into constellation diagrams, which were then mapped into graph structures. The method significantly improved classification accuracy for low, medium, and high order modulation schemes by extracting edge information and applying feature mapping within convolutional blocks. Similarly, [32] proposed an enhanced relational graph convolutional network (RGCN) to extract semantic features of modulation techniques. By aggregating and separating signal features, the model greatly improved classification performance and interpretability, especially under low SNR conditions.
Another approach involves treating signal sampling points as graphic nodes and mapping time series data into the graph structure before feeding it into a Graph Convolutional Network (GCN). For example, in [33], the Adaptive Visual Attention Graph (AVG) is used to adaptively map time series signals into the graph, and the classification performance of radio signal modulation tasks has been significantly improved with the support of the improved GCN model DiffPool. In [34], the modulation signal sampling points are mapped onto graphic nodes, and the local and global signal dependencies are modeled using the time-frequency structure and weighted edges. This achieves AMR under various SNR conditions. In [35], a hybrid deep learning model named LAGNet is introduced, which combines a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network for time feature extraction and an attention mechanism that maps data into the graph structure, followed by a GCN to extract spatial features. Additionally, Liu et al. [36] proposed a modulation recognition method based on GCN, where a CNN (FECNN) for feature extraction and a Graph Mapping CNN (GMCNN) for mapping subsets of modulation signals into the graph structure are designed. Then, the GCN extracts graph features, thereby improving the accuracy of modulation recognition, especially under low SNR conditions.
However, the above methods can disrupt the temporal sequence, continuity, and local correlation of the time series signal when converting it into a graph structure. This results in insufficient preservation of original signal features, impacting the model's recognition performance. Especially under low SNR conditions, noise can interfere with the definition of edges and nodes in the graph structure, making it challenging to effectively extract key features.
To overcome the limitations of existing methods, this paper proposes an AMR method based on Adaptive Relational Graph Attention Network (ARGAT). The main contributions and innovations of this paper are as follows.
The paper introduces a new method for designing graph node feature matrices using 2-D time series reordering. It proposes a novel approach to preserve temporal features when mapping time series signals into graph nodes. By constructing the feature matrix with the same type of time-domain signal under the same SNR conditions but with different parameter settings, this method retains the sequence, continuity, and local correlation of the signals.
Adaptive Weighted Assignment Acceleration Covariance Calculation (ASCC): We present an ASCC method for adaptive weighted assignment. By integrating mutual and Pearson correlation coefficients (PCC), ASCC captures both time shifts and amplitude dependencies. Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) is used to accelerate mutual correlation, while Randomized Principal Component Analysis (RPCA) is employed for noise reduction and dimensionality reduction. This method significantly improves accuracy in low SNR environments, consistently outperforming PCC across all tested SNR levels.
Recurrent Feedforward Neural Networks with ARGAT for Signal Recognition: We introduce the ARGAT model, which combines an improved graph convolution layer (G Layer), a graph attention layer (GAT), and a transposed graph convolution layer (T Layer). This architecture captures local and global relationships through self-attention, achieving over 91% accuracy at −14 dB SNR and outperforming ResNet and DenseNet in low SNR signal recognition.

Conclusion
This paper introduces the ARGAT model for AMR of RFPS signals, aiming to address the challenges posed by typical low SNR conditions in RFPS signals. We propose a two-dimensional graph node feature matrix to preserve time-domain information crucial for signal recognition and design an ASCC to achieve adaptive weighting between graph nodes. The architecture of the ARGAT model combines an improved GAT for graph convolution and a transposed graph convolution layer to effectively extract both local and global features. Experiments show that the ARGAT achieves over 91% classification accuracy at -14 dB SNR, surpassing existing models such as ResNet and DenseNet. Additionally, the performance of ASCC consistently outperforms PCC under low SNR conditions.
In future work, our goal is to optimize the node allocation strategy of the ARGAT to improve AMR performance while minimizing the number of network nodes and computational resource requirements. This optimization will enhance model efficiency, making it more suitable for real-time applications with limited resources. Moreover, we plan to conduct experiments in real transmission environments to study the completion of the research, better meeting the needs of practical applications.
